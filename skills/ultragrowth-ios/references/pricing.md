# Pricing and paywalls

From RevenueCat's *State of Subscription Apps 2026*: 115,000 apps,
$16B of revenue.

## The findings worth acting on

- **Hard paywalls convert about 5x better than freemium**, and long-term
  retention is similar. The common fear that a hard paywall burns the
  funnel is mostly not supported. If you are freemium out of nervousness
  rather than strategy, test it.
- **Short trials pull revenue forward and cost long-term conversion.**
  Do not shorten a trial to make a quarter look better.
- **Median MRR growth was 5.3% year on year. The top decile grew 306%+,
  the bottom decile shrank.** This is a winner-take-most market and the
  median is a warning rather than a target.
- **New subscription apps per month went from ~2,000 to ~15,000 in three
  years.** Quality is table stakes. Findability is the work.

## Choosing a model

| Shape | Usually right |
| --- | --- |
| Ongoing cost to you (servers, data, API) | Subscription |
| Ongoing *value* to them (new content, sync) | Subscription |
| A thing somebody wants to own | One-off, or one-off with optional pro |
| Something used a handful of times a year | One-off. A subscription will churn and resent you |

Do not subscribe a calculator.

## Paywall placement

Let people reach the value, then ask. "Show value, then ask for
investment" is the one thing every source agrees on, across both viral
and non-viral products. The disagreement is only about how much value.

## Free tier design

If there is a free tier it should be **complete for a real use**, not a
demo with the ending removed. A free tier that is obviously crippled
converts worse than a hard paywall *and* earns one-star reviews, which
cost you conversions you already paid for.

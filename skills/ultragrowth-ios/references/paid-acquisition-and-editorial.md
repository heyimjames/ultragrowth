# Apple Search Ads, and getting featured

The two things small apps most often either skip entirely or attempt
badly.

## Search Ads: the arithmetic first

Apple's own guidance sets the floor: budget for **at least five
conversions a day**, and run **at least two weeks** before judging. At US
costs per install that is roughly $249/month for Education, $441 for
Utilities, $476 for Productivity, $1,283 for Finance.

Median US cost per install, 2025, search results placement:

| Category | CPI |
| --- | --- |
| Education | $1.64 |
| Utilities | $2.90 |
| Productivity | $3.13 |
| Health and Fitness | $3.83 |
| Finance | $8.44 |
| Sports | $26.81 |

Now the other side of it. Median **revenue per install** across
subscription apps is **$0.23 at day 14 and $0.34 at day 60**. A median
freemium Health and Fitness app paying $3.83 a install recovers about
$1.03 in the first year, which is **losing about 73% of the spend**.

That is the whole decision. Search Ads clears for a small app only with a
hard paywall, a high price point, or unusually good retention. If you are
median on monetisation, it does not clear, and no amount of campaign
tuning fixes an order of magnitude.

### If you do run it

- **Search results is the only placement worth a small budget.** Tap
  through 7.4% and conversion 56%, against the Today tab at 0.32% and
  7.7%, which is roughly eight times worse on effective cost.
- **Two ads per search query went live in March 2026** and advertisers
  cannot bid on position. Any brand-defence advice written before then is
  stale.
- **On organic spillover, be honest about the evidence.** The only
  credible quasi-experiment is an ad-shutoff study where organic installs
  fell 20 to 30%. It was games, in 2018 to 2019, across all channels
  combined, measuring Top Charts rather than keyword rank. There is **no
  controlled evidence that Search Ads lifts organic rank on the keyword
  you bid on**, and Apple's ranking statement does not distinguish paid
  from organic downloads.

## Editorial featuring: the free lever

Apple's published list of what it looks for, verbatim: user experience,
UI design, innovation, uniqueness, accessibility, localisation, and the
App Store product page.

**Lead time.** Apple states four different numbers across four pages: 2
weeks minimum, 3 weeks, 6 to 8 weeks "for best results", and up to 3
months for wider consideration. Plan on **6 to 8 weeks**.

**The Featuring Nominations form** in App Store Connect is the actual
mechanism and most developers have never opened it. 1,000 characters of
description, 500 of helpful details, and up to five supplemental URLs
which **explicitly accept TestFlight public links** ,  so you can nominate
something before it ships.

**In-App Events are a second editorial queue**, and one you can re-enter
without shipping a version. Up to 31 days, promotable 14 days ahead, ten
published at once.

### What being featured is actually worth

The most useful firsthand non-game account is Curtis Herbert's, for
Slopes, App of the Day in January 2018:

- ~19,000 downloads on the day, ~35,000 total
- against 120,000 in the entire preceding year
- a seasonal list placement a month earlier gave only ~2,800
- and **only about 5,500 of the 19,000 ever launched the app**

That last line is the one to keep. A feature is a spike in downloads, not
a spike in users, and if your first run cannot convert a curious tap into
an activation you are converting a once-in-a-lifetime event into a
rounding error.

Relative values by feature type, from one vendor's median week on week
study: Game of the Day +802%, App of the Day +685%, App Lists +240%, App
Stories +222%. Same study: **29% of featured apps came from publishers
with under 10,000 downloads**, which is the encouraging part.

Apple recognition does compound: Design Award finalists become App of the
Year, and appearing in a technology gallery precedes a Design Award.

But be careful with the common claim that editors reward adopting Apple's
newest frameworks. **Widgets, Live Activities, App Intents and the
Dynamic Island appear nowhere in Apple's seven published criteria**, and
the nomination form has no technology field at all. Apple defines the
"helpful details" box as being about values, accessibility and the team's
story, not technology. Apple *does* say novel use of its technologies
matters, but it says it about the **Apple Design Awards**, which is a
different programme with different judges. Do not ship a widget to get
featured. Ship it because it is useful.

### What nobody can tell you

- No firsthand account exists publicly of the Nominations form actually
  producing a feature. Apple's claim that editors read every submission
  is the only evidence either way.
- No developer has published before and after numbers for a Design Award.
- There is no per-feature-type download data after 2018, and no evidence
  of durable ranking benefit beyond about two weeks.

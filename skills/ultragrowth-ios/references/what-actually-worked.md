# What actually worked, with the numbers, from the people it worked for

Firsthand accounts only. Every figure here was published by the developer
who earned it, not by a vendor summarising them.

The pattern across all of them is worth seeing before the detail: **not
one of these grew through Apple Search Ads.** Two tried it and stopped.

## Shot Pattern: a niche utility, and Meta rather than Apple

A golf strategy app at $20 a year.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Total sales when API costs were $5,000 | **$1,000** |
| Total sales when offered $75,000 to sell | $12,000 |
| Profit by end of 2024 | **over $75,000** |
| Total sales by early 2026 | **over $1,000,000** |
| Cost per install, Meta ads | **$0.80** |
| 30-day LTV | **$4+** |

The cleanest published unit economics for a non-social utility anywhere.
Two things to take from it. The channel that worked was **Meta, not Apple
Search Ads**, at a fifth of the cost per install that Search Ads charges
for Utilities. And the creative that worked was **deliberately
unpolished**, which is worth remembering before commissioning anything.

## Short Circuit: one press hit, priced

Built in nineteen days.

- **$5,000 in the first two hours**
- **$10,000 in 36 hours**
- from **a single Daring Fireball placement**

That is a concrete price tag for one well-aimed piece of press in the
Apple niche, and it is why "pitch the two or three outlets your actual
buyers read" beats a broad press push for a small app.

## Sequel: the other end of the distribution

- **$1,000 in revenue across eight months**
- **$100 spent on Apple Search Ads, then the channel abandoned**
- A MacStories review was the turning point, download number undisclosed
- The developer's own kill criterion was £1,000 in the first month of
  v2.0, or go and get a job

Publish this one alongside the wins. Most apps are here, and the useful
detail is that the thing that moved it was a review in a publication its
buyers read, not advertising.

## Sindre Sorhus: paid-upfront, at scale, in 2026

Sixty-three apps over eleven years. Six million users. **Thirty-three of
them one-time purchase.** No subscriptions, not on Setapp.

No revenue figure published, so treat it as an existence proof rather
than a benchmark: a paid-upfront portfolio is still operating at scale,
against every piece of advice saying it cannot be.

## Activation, where two people did publish real numbers

**Slopes, on charging for a trial.** Of about 35,000 premium customers,
roughly 2,500 bought the $1.99 Day Pass for its intended purpose and
**about 5,200, or 20%, bought it purely as a way to try the app**. The
developer estimated a real free trial would convert two to four times
better, projecting a 10 to 15% revenue increase with a worst case of
minus 8%.

A fifth of his buyers were paying to evaluate. If you make people pay to
find out, some will, and you will never know how many did not.

**Natal, on removing the free trial from one tier only.** Removed from
monthly, kept elsewhere:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly subscriptions | **+2,000%** |
| Quarterly | +46% |
| Annual | +21% |
| Trial conversion | **68%**, against a 38% category baseline |
| Download to trial | **17%**, against a 6% baseline |

The lesson is not "remove your trial". It is that trial structure is a
per-tier decision and almost nobody treats it as one.

## Visible: $1M to $10M without most of the playbook

Two years, cashflow positive, and explicitly **no paywall testing, no
lifecycle email, no SEO, no TikTok, no AdWords**.

- **50% paid acquisition, 50% word of mouth**
- **One channel only**: Meta plus user-generated content
- **Median ten-day consideration period** from landing page to purchase

The ten days matters: for a considered purchase the job of the landing
page is not to convert today, it is to be findable again on day nine.

And the broader point, which is the whole of `SKILL.md` in one example:
they found one channel that fitted the product and did it properly,
rather than doing nine things adequately.
